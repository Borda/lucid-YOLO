    📐 Phase 17 — R1 pose-objective parity¶

    WP-178 — the pose section was there all along¶

    A false premise, found from outside. Issue 2 on the tracker reports what the record should have caught itself: R1 sec. 3.4.2 is a pose-estimation section — a sigmoid-bounded sigma branch, Eq. 10's normalized residual, Eq. 11's RLE term and an OKS-based loss the two are combined with — Table 9 ablates the OKS/RLE split and Table S10 publishes every pose scale's size, all inside R1's allowlisted role. D21, A65, A68, _build_keypoint_stem's docstring, test_param_flops.py's comment, the WP-139 entry above and both keypoint sections of the reproduction report say R1 has no such section. The citation map shows how: sec. 3.4.1 and 3.4.3 are cited across the tree, Eq. 7–9 and 13–15 likewise, and nothing between them — the gap was visible without opening the paper, and model_cards/keypoints.md had quoted Table 9's 63.0 since 0.5.0 while calling it a different paper's number. Confirmed 2026-09-17 against the arXiv PDF, not against the issue's paraphrase.

    What changes and what does not. D22 withdraws D21's rationale clause and nothing else — the tier stays keypoints-not-pose, classification and YOLOE-26 stay out — and states what shipped as a partial reproduction of R1's objective: Eq. 11's RLE term, transcribed from R14 where R1 itself draws it, without the OKS term. In the registers and logs — DECISIONS, ASSUMPTIONS, this log, PROVENANCE — every stale sentence is struck in place with the date, on D21's own reasoning about records; the reproduction report, append-only under D10, gets a correcting note after its last section and its 0.5.0 section stays byte-identical; the model card, two docstrings and a test comment are rewritten outright with a dated note, since none of those is a record of a decision. Three findings the section forces are registered rather than resolved. A75: R1 gives the OKS loss no formula and cites it to a paper outside the allowlist, so losses/oks_loss.py derives 1 - OKS from R12's own definition — box area for s^2, since a keypoints batch carries no segment area — and enters at oks_gain = 0.0, keeping the shipped objective bit-exact and WP-125's paired-run evidence meaningful; Table 9's (24, 1) is the published split no run here has tested. A76: R1's printed Eq. 11 carries log sigma twice, once as the reparameterization Jacobian and once inside a Laplace term written in the un-normalized frame, where R14 Eq. 8 — what the code carries — has it once; the two differ by a 1/sigma gradient on every scale, and which R1 means is not settled by its text, so the form stays and the paired run that would settle it is a roadmap item. A77: measured at Table S10's protocol the point stem is 13.0% / 26.5% under the paper's n params / FLOPs and converges by l/x — the same finding A20 made for the angle stem before its width was chosen against Table S11, and it gets the same treatment: recorded, gate deferred until a width is selected against the table, nothing tuned in a correction commit.

    What the term costs switched off. _score_branch gathers each branch's assigned positives once and scores them with both terms, so the added cost at oks_gain = 0 is one OKS evaluation per branch per step over already-gathered tensors — an exponential over the labelled points, no decode and no second argsort. It is logged pre-gain as train/oks for the same reason every other term is, so a run can see the number it is not yet optimizing.

    The lesson is a check, not a resolution. Before the record says a source does not cover something, grep what the record already cites from that source and look for the hole: two adjacent subsections cited and the one between them never is not an absence in the paper. .notes/lessons.md carries it as a rule.

    WP-183 — the box terms scored densely, so the step traces as one graph¶

    A boolean gather is a shape the compiler cannot know. pred_boxes[fg_mask] lowers to aten.nonzero, whose output length is a property of the data, and torch.compile answers that with a graph break; the detection step carried seven of them, at the masked gathers. Recorded 2026-09-18. The replacement evaluates every term over the full (B, A) anchor grid — CIoU through a reshape(-1, 4) since its contract is two-dimensional, ProbIoU directly since it broadcasts, the L1 differences against the dense stride, the angle term over the whole branch — and selects the positives afterwards. The selection is a torch.where, not a multiply by the mask: a background anchor's target is the all-zero box, so the dense CIoU there is scored on a degenerate pair, and 0 * NaN is still NaN where where is an exact selection in both the value and the backward pass. What remains is a dense sum in which every off-positive term is an exact zero, and square_angle_loss already documents whole-branch input with q_i = 0 background as its intended second use.

    Loss-identical, measured rather than argued. On a COCO batch the two forms differ by an absolute 0.0 on every term, the gradients stay finite on an image with no positives, and the pre-change CPU step snapshot holds at its existing tolerance; in eager the step is 2% faster, with the seven nonzero calls gone. The masked implementation is kept verbatim as a private oracle in each test module, and the dense form is held against it on random assignments — an image with no positives, an all-background batch, and degenerate predictions (zero-area, inverted, all-zero, both sides collapsed) planted on background anchors — to 1e-6 on values and gradients. The oriented oracle draws gt_index from [0, N) with N ≥ 2, so the gather over the padded instance axis is exercised on every column rather than only its first. WP-184 is what the one-graph step is for.

    What the accelerator gate said afterwards (2026-09-19). On an A100-40GB, two of the five goldens/gpu/ rows moved out of band under this change alone: keypoints 0.5696 → 0.4919 and oriented 0.9783 → 0.9088, per-commit on the same machine, with WP-182's layout change accounting for −0.021 / −0.040 of the path and detection, segmentation and shapes_regression_det holding. The arithmetic is the same; the summation order is not, and the overfit gate is a 100-epoch trajectory that amplifies a 1e-7 per-step difference into a different endpoint — the same sensitivity the WP-177 escalation measured across seeds on unchanged code. The principal resealed both rows at the measured values (ESCALATION 2026-09-19, WP-186); the oriented band is now clamped_tolerance-narrow at ± 0.0088.

    And what three seeds said (2026-09-20). The control the escalation asked for, run per commit on the same A100: the oriented move that looked monotone at HEAD splits into WP-182's −0.020 (3 of 3 seeds down) and this change's −0.014 (2 of 3), and keypoints span 0.49–0.73 across nine runs of the same objective. A layout change with no arithmetic in it producing the same signature as the loss rewrite is the evidence that the signature is the gate's, not the change's. Full table in ESCALATION 2026-09-19's follow-up.

    WP-188 — notebooks as sources, the .ipynb as a build product¶

    The format question was decided by what the gate can read. Recorded 2026-09-21. A notebook is worth publishing only while it still runs, and nothing about publishing one checks that; so the first decision was that every notebook is a .py the existing machinery already handles -- ruff lints it, license-headers scopes it, and tests/notebooks/test_notebooks_run.py runs it as a script under LUCID_NOTEBOOK_FAST=1 on every make gate. Jupytext's percent format is the one that costs nothing: a # %% line is a comment to Python and a cell to the converter. The .ipynb follows from the .py at build time in two places, docs/notebooks/ for the site and notebooks/<name>.ipynb on gh-pages for the Colab badge, and neither is ever committed to main; no-ipynb-tracked reads the index on every commit because .gitignore is advice and git add -f ignores it (D25). The script run ruled out one thing: the Colab bootstrap could not be a !git clone line, because jupytext writes a magic into the .py as a comment and a guarded block of comments alone is a SyntaxError when the file runs as a script -- so the bootstrap, and after it every console command the demo notebooks ran, went through subprocess.

    The script run is withdrawn; the gate executes the notebook through a kernel. Revised 2026-09-21, once the four demo notebooks existed. Each of them is a sequence of console commands -- lucid-data download, lucid-yolo fit, lucid-eval, lucid-predict -- and as subprocess.run([...]) lists they read as argv arrays, not as the command lines docs/TRAINING.md spells. The user's ask was that they read as plain shell, and that decides the gate's mechanism rather than the other way round: each command is a %%bash cell -- # %% language="bash" in the .py with the script commented beneath, comments to a script run -- so the proof moves from "run the .py" to "convert the .py and execute the .ipynb with a kernel". The test now runs jupytext --from py:percent --to ipynb --execute --set-kernel python3 --run-path <repo root> per source, the same conversion make notebooks makes plus execution, and the subprocess reasoning above no longer applies because nothing runs the file as a script any more; the bootstrap is !git clone and %pip install -e lucid-YOLO under the same google.colab guard, with os.chdir and the sys.path insert keeping the block a block. Values cross into a shell cell through the environment -- the Python cell above does os.environ.update(DATA_ROOT=..., FIT_ARGS=...), the fast budget being one such string of flags -- and a switch the shell itself has to make reads $LUCID_NOTEBOOK_FAST, so the download the fast run skips and the lucid-predict loop only the fast run makes are if blocks in bash beside the command they guard. Three things were measured rather than assumed. A ! line does not fail the notebook -- !false followed by a print printed, under nbclient, with the cell green, IPython storing the status in _exit_code and continuing -- which is why the commands are %%bash cells and not ! lines: the script magic raises CalledProcessError on the cell's exit status and nbclient stops, as a probe with false confirmed; that status is the last command's, so a cell with more than one opens with set -e. --set-kernel -, jupytext's spelling for "the current interpreter", raises No kernel found that matches the current python executable: the kernelspec ipykernel's wheel installs into .venv/share/jupyter/kernels/python3 has argv[0] equal to the bare word python, and jupytext compares that to sys.executable with os.path.samefile. --set-kernel python3 names that spec directly, and jupyter_client.manager.KernelManager.format_kernel_cmd swaps a bare python for the launcher's sys.executable -- the venv's, since pytest runs jupytext as python -m jupytext -- which a probe confirmed from inside the kernel; so there is no ipykernel install step, and the test sets JUPYTER_PATH to the venv's share/jupyter so a user-level python3 spec, which the search order otherwise puts first, cannot take the name. And --from py:percent is not optional: jupytext's guess_format counts an indented # !git clone -- the Colab block's -- as an unescaped magic, because its escaped-magic test is not line.startswith("#") with no lstrip, and guesses hydrogen, a format that leaves every # ! line a comment. A jupytext header after the SPDX line and a [tool.jupytext] table in pyproject.toml were both tried and neither reaches the CLI reader, so the flag went into the test, the Makefile and docs.yml together; without it the published .ipynb would carry # !git clone as a comment and the badge would open a notebook that clones nothing. The dev group gains jupytext, nbconvert and ipykernel -- nbconvert and not nbclient, because jupytext --execute imports nbconvert.preprocessors.ExecutePreprocessor and declares neither as a hard requirement -- and the licence audit on the dev environment stayed clean at 152 distributions. Under LUCID_NOTEBOOK_FAST=1 the five sources take 18–32 s each through the kernel on an Apple-silicon CPU (the four-task wiring gate the 32 s), kernel start included, against the test's 60 s ceiling, which therefore did not move. The full runs' paths were settled at the same time: a dataset goes under the gitignored data/ of the checkout -- data/coco2017 for the three COCO demos, data/dota and data/dota_tiles for the oriented one -- and the run under results/demo_<task>, the Colab detect demo keeping its Drive guidance; the fast run keeps its temporary directory.

    The demos moved off the shell cells; the kernel run stays. Revised again 2026-09-21, once the four demos were rewritten onto the library: every step is now a call on an object the kernel holds -- download_coco, DetectionLitModule and DetectionDataModule built from the tier's YAML values, Trainer.fit and Trainer.validate, lucid_yolo.predict and lucid_yolo._viz -- and the console command is quoted as prose, never run, so the %%bash cells, the os.environ hand-off and the $LUCID_NOTEBOOK_FAST test in bash are gone from them. The three measurements above still stand as measurements, they just no longer bear on the demos. What keeps the gate on jupytext --execute through a kernel rather than a script run is the bootstrap: !git clone and %pip install -e lucid-YOLO are magics, written into the .py as comments, and a script run would read them as comments and prove nothing about the two lines a Colab reader depends on; the --from py:percent reasoning is theirs as well. One consequence for a figure cell: its last expression is None if FAST else prediction_grid(640), because a bare name as the cell's last line is B018 to ruff and a call is not. Measured on an Apple-silicon CPU, the four demos take about 11 s each under the fast contract, against 18-32 s through the console scripts. The gate notebook went the other way for the same reason, one file later: the four overfit commands and the shapes regression are development tooling with no library entry point, so notebooks/development_gates.py runs them as the ! lines TRAINING.md spells, $PROBE appended under the fast contract -- --epochs 1 --batches 4, a probe override the overfit script gained on the shapes script's model, reporting PROBE with the score and enforcing no floor; 26 s for all five commands.

    What the scratch-venv audit found. The docs group alone resolves to 43 distributions with jupytext added and 77 with mkdocs-jupyter, the 34 between them being nbconvert, nbclient, ipykernel and what they pull; pyzmq (BSD-3-Clause) is the one shipping a native binary the audit's wheel scan walks. Licence audit clean at both counts, nothing copyleft. docs.yml's build job is the only CI environment holding this tree, and its audit step already runs before the build, so the exposure is checked where it enters. The site is rendered with execute: false, so that job stays torch-free, which is what keeps the docs group small enough to audit by hand as well.

    The --strict risk was checked with a throwaway notebook and did not fire. The plan flagged that mkdocs-jupyter might warn on a converted file missing kernel metadata, which --strict would turn into a failure. A percent-format source with one markdown cell and one code cell, converted by jupytext and listed in the nav, built strict with no warning and rendered its cells; jupytext writes the kernelspec. The source and its .ipynb were deleted after the build. Nothing in the shipped tree depends on this, since WP-188 lands zero notebooks -- it is recorded so WP-189 does not re-derive it.

    Two checks moved from the site to the audit, and one rule changed shape. check_every_nav_entry_points_at_a_file_that_exists used to ask whether the nav target was a file under docs/; a notebooks/<name>.ipynb target now resolves against notebooks/<name>.py instead, whether or not the .ipynb has been written yet. The alternative -- accept the entry when the generated file is on disk -- would have answered differently on a warm checkout and a cold one, and a checker whose verdict depends on whether make notebooks was run is a checker that passes locally and fails in CI. check_every_notebook_source_has_a_nav_entry is the page rule (a register nobody can navigate to is one nobody reads) applied from the source side, and it tolerates a mkdocs.yml with no Notebooks section while no source exists, because MkDocs rejects a nav section with no children and the section therefore cannot arrive before the first notebook does.

    The badge decided how the site is published. The first draft of this row added a publish-notebooks job writing the .ipynb files to an orphan notebooks branch, beside the existing Pages artifact deploy -- two publishing mechanisms for one site. The revision (2026-09-21) collapses them: the site itself now goes out as a branch deploy, ghp-import -n -p committing site/ onto gh-pages on top of that branch's history, and the converted notebooks are copied to site/notebooks/<name>.ipynb before the import so the badge's blob/gh-pages/notebooks/<name>.ipynb is a path on the branch Pages serves. mkdocs-jupyter's include_source places its own copy at site/notebooks/<name>/<name>.ipynb, beside the rendered page; the flat copy is the badge's, and both ride the same commit. ghp-import arrives with mkdocs, so the docs group gains nothing for it; deploy-pages, upload-pages-artifact, configure-pages and the pages/id-token permissions leave the workflow, and contents: write sits on the one job that remains -- a pull-request run holds it too, since a job's grant is fixed for the job, and never reaches the step that uses it. The Makefile and both workflow loops iterate a shell glob with an existence test rather than $(wildcard), since for f in ; is a syntax error under /bin/sh and an empty notebooks/ is the state this row ships in.

    WP-189 — the drawing code moves into the package, and the dependency it already had is declared¶

    Four notebooks would have carried four copies. Recorded 2026-09-21. WP-188 made notebooks/*.py gate-run sources, and the first four notebooks each want two figures: a run's curves and a checkpoint's predictions over a few images, labels beside them. Both existed — scripts/plot_training.py drew the first and scripts/draw_predictions.py the second — and a notebook cannot import a script, so each notebook would have re-spelled the drawers inline. That is the failure the WP-067 entry names from the other direction: a notebook nothing tests rots, and drawing code copied into four of them rots four ways at once, each copy free to draw the rotated box as its envelope again. So the functional core of both scripts moved to src/lucid_yolo/_viz/ — curves.py for the panels and the CSV reader, overlay.py for the four per-task drawers, the renderer table and the checkpoint-to-axes path — and each script is now its argument parse, its backend selection and its write. The notebook entry points are two: plot_curves(metrics) returns the report's figure titled by the run directory, and show_predictions(checkpoint, images, annotations) loads the checkpoint once, draws one panel per image, and lays the labels under the predictions when an annotation file is given; a notebook that already holds the trained module draws each panel with predict_and_draw(module, image, options, axes=ax) and draw_ground_truth(ax, targets, task) instead, the skeleton for a pose grid travelling in RenderOptions.skeleton so the four tasks share one call shape. Every public name the scripts published stays importable from them as a re-export, so scripts/_tests/test_draw_predictions.py keeps its command-level cases and the drawing cases moved to tests/viz/, where the code they assert now lives.

    Ground truth is dashed, unfilled, and read by the training reader. The new drawer, draw_ground_truth(axes, targets, task), draws the label layer in the task's own geometry — boxes, instance rings, rotated quadrilaterals through the same rboxes_to_polygons the predicted rings use, boxes with a hollow ring at each visible point — and never fills or writes a score, so a prediction drawn solid on the same axes stays tellable from its target without a legend. What it takes is a Targets, which is what CocoDetectionDataset yields per image in the reading mode the task needs (oriented for obb, keypoints for keypoints); the file is not parsed a second time. The dataset needed two accessors for that — file_names, the companion of image_ids for a caller who arrives with a file rather than an id, and targets_at(index), the precomputed targets without the image decode __getitem__ performs — and both are the WP's one edit outside _viz, the scripts and the tests.

    Private, and matplotlib is a runtime dependency — because it already was. The WP-067 paragraph kept the drawers out of the package on the claim that matplotlib sits in the dev group and "a wheel a consumer installs pulls no plotting stack". Checked here rather than repeated: pytorch-lightning[extra], in [project].dependencies, requires matplotlib>3.1 (importlib.metadata.requires("pytorch-lightning") lists it under extra == "extra"), so every consumer install has pulled matplotlib since that extra was declared, and torchmetrics imports it the moment it finds it installed. The claim was false before this row and the row changes nothing about what a consumer receives. What it does change is the declaration: matplotlib>=3.8 moves from the dev group to [project].dependencies, on the jsonargparse and tqdm precedent already in that list — a direct import is not a transitive dependency — and because WP-168's release guard reads declarations, not the transitive graph: check_dependency_tiers refused the tree with _viz importing a group-only distribution, and five scripts/_tests/test_release_guard.py cases (test_happy_path_passes, test_main_without_tag_falls_back_to_head, test_metadata_only_leaves_gate_command_unrun, test_certifying_run_invokes_gate_exactly_once, test_metadata_only_verdict_refuses_to_call_tag_shippable) failed on the live tree until it moved. The package is still _viz, underscore first, and the underscore is now an API contract rather than a dependency one: nothing else in lucid_yolo imports it, nothing outside it imports matplotlib, and the notebooks are its only callers, so its signatures can move with them. tests/viz/test_import_surface.py pins that from two sides. The dynamic side imports the shipped surface — lucid_yolo, lucid_yolo.predict, lucid_yolo.eval.checkpoint, lucid_yolo.data — in a fresh interpreter and asserts lucid_yolo._viz never entered sys.modules; the static side reads every shipped module's source and refuses an import line naming matplotlib or _viz outside the _viz subtree, which is the check that catches a function-local import no import of the surface executes. Neither asserts matplotlib absent from sys.modules after the import: torchmetrics loads it (torchmetrics/utilities/plot.py), and that is theirs.

    No backend is selected in the package. The plan had _viz keep the scripts' matplotlib.use("Agg") at module top. It does not: use("Agg") is a process-wide setting, and a notebook importing a package that makes it is switched away from its inline display — figures returned from a cell would stop rendering. So the backend selection stays in the two scripts and the two test conftests, which are the headless callers, and the package imports pyplot unconditionally with no use() before it. For the same reason build_run_figure, plot_curves, predict_and_draw and show_predictions return their figure open: the script saves and closes, a notebook displays, and closing inside the package would serve the first by breaking the second. The svg.hashsalt moved with the backend line, since it is the committed figure's concern and the same kind of global.

    WP-192 — the complete oriented evaluator uses the worker policy it already had¶

    Timing evidence is host-specific and non-gated. The off-repository procedures were profiling the public scoring loop, comparing five-run medians with channels_last, traversing the loader, and calling public run; the warm-up and repetition procedure is recorded below where available. The benchmark harness and raw output are not committed.

    The standalone path predates the image-size target and inherited-validation guard. The evaluator's explicit num_workers=0 arrived in 0fe08f4 on 12 August. The free-/dev/shm cap on automatic workers was already present from 9f1fa80 on 2 August; the image-size validation worker target (9d0fa88) and inherited-validation guard (d09ded9) arrived on 13 August, one day after the call site. 00af9e4 on 6 September renamed _val_worker_cap to _val_worker_target and clarified its documentation without changing the policy. Nothing revisited the call site, so automatic worker selection could only govern datamodules whose callers left worker selection open. Through the inherited-validation guard, the evaluator's explicit zero was raised to one validation worker wherever /dev/shm exists. All measured "existing route" baselines below, including 0.850 s, 9.757 s and 13.104 s, therefore used one validation worker; the current low-startup route runs zero workers and is unmeasured. A profile of the public scoring loop over 117 real DOTA tiles spent 3.60 of 5.10 s waiting for the loader. The rotated accumulator took 0.051 s, so even removing it entirely could save only 1.0% of that run. Converting the standalone module to channels_last, the other live candidate, moved the five-run median from 2.679 to 2.645 s and overlapped both ranges; it was rejected.

    Completeness is not itself the crossover. Starting eight workers to score only eight tiles took a 1.784 s median against 0.850 s on the existing route. A positive --limit therefore keeps that route unconditionally — the measurement is the smallest size the diagnostic path runs at, not a sweep, so a large positive --limit keeps the same route by scope rather than by a remeasured crossover. With no limit, the evaluator asks the datamodule for its existing policy, then keeps it only when the loader has more batches than workers: otherwise no process can be reused after its first batch. That structural guard covers the measured one-batch regression and a complete but tiny custom split without introducing a dataset-size constant. No crossover measurement was taken on macOS / MPS, where workers spawn rather than fork, and the band of batch counts just above the worker count is also unmeasured. Nothing in the reader, letterbox, model, decoder, merge or metric changes. Tests walk run through both public branches and the tiny-complete fallback, while a separate real-pool case reads the same twelve tiles through one and two workers and compares images, boxes, labels, rotated boxes, difficult flags, order and the complete report.

    The loader result was repeated on an isolated CPU. Five measured traversals after one warm-up on an n2-standard-8 Ice Lake VM (Python 3.12.3, torch 2.14.1) moved from a 9.757 s median (9.651–9.987 s) with one validation worker to 2.658 s (2.609–2.781 s) with the policy's eight, a 72.96% median reduction. Every traversal yielded the same 117 tiles, 2,691 instances and tensor checksum. That figure is the loader alone: CPU-device inference was not measured. The policy's worker count derives from os.cpu_count(), which is blind to container CPU quotas and process affinity, so a host that limits either may start more workers than it can run. A quota- or affinity-aware count would change the datamodule policy that training shares and belongs in its own work package, not in WP-192.

    The complete pipeline keeps the gain. Five measured public run calls after one warm-up on an NVIDIA L4 (driver 580.178.04, torch 2.14.1+cu130, batch 8) included loading, model inference, rotated decoding and scoring, whole-image merge and scoring, and report construction.     WP-193b — compatible matrix batches and momentum dispatch¶

    Compatible matrix batches and momentum dispatch. One diagnostic session after the native-batch and image-cache changes attributed a 49.28 ms median to the optimizer within a 159.60 ms complete detection interval on an isolated L4. Its 10.90 ms momentum-dispatch median suggested a 6.8% stage share; this is an exploratory opportunity estimate, not an independently repeated speedup or a universal ceiling. The combined candidate targets the remaining launch overhead within the same optimizer phase.

    Zero extension changes the batch, not the quintic. For a wide matrix X, appending zero columns preserves its Frobenius norm and X @ X.T; every iterate of the existing polynomial therefore keeps those columns zero. CUDA matrices sharing the smaller dimension can run in one native batch, then crop and transpose back before their original dimension-dependent scale, gains and decay. Each padded gradient stack is bounded by the parameter and gradient bytes of the matrices in its own batch. A singleton, or a batch whose matrices all share one shape, keeps the exact-shape update; equal-shape matrices that share a smaller dimension with a matrix of another width join the padded batch. Parameters whose memory ranges overlap keep exact-shape keys, whichever storage object wraps them. No coefficient, iteration count or normalization rule changes.

    Momentum keeps its rounded operations. Three foreach operations replace the CUDA per-parameter multiply, add and Nesterov add, with the original operation order. Device and dtype buckets keep independent state. Shared parameter/gradient/buffer storage and unusual restored state use the original scalar method; CPU and MPS keep their previous implementation. No public flag or checkpoint field is added.

    The numerical contract is bounded agreement. Mixed-width GEMMs and reductions may round differently. A real one-step COCO run and its baseline-against-itself control have the same loss bytes, all 369 momentum buffers match bit for bit, and the largest model/buffer difference is 2.98e-8. A 60-step zero-warmup screen diverges in its BF16 trajectory, so this is not a claim of identical stochastic training or final accuracy. The mixed-shape scalar-oracle, storage-budget, alias, DLPack-overlap, dtype, checkpoint-resume and foreach recurrence tests cover the altered scheduling. The guarded screen improved the optimizer in all five scales, but those stage numbers are not the training claim. Parameter subclasses may legitimately refuse storage inspection: a baseline-compatible vector subclass exposed that regression before handoff. Such groups now keep their original keys and scalar momentum, including mixed groups where opaque storage may alias an ordinary parameter.

    WP-197b — static matrix iterations keep their arithmetic and lose host dispatch¶

    The captured region is pure. capture_orthogonalization, off by default, records the existing scalar and batched Newton--Schulz functions rather than the optimizer step. Each new matrix is copied in and each returned result is copied out. Momentum, parameter writes, learning-rate schedules, gradient scaling and EMA stay outside the graphs. Neither function's arithmetic changes. CPU, MPS, autograd, autocast, non-contiguous and offset inputs keep the eager functions; an enclosing capture or compiler also keeps the original operations.

    The optimizer owns the workspace. The cache is bounded by its matrix-parameter count. Function, shape, stride, dtype, device, step count, epsilon, CUDA stream, GEMM precision, BLAS backend and determinism select a recording. New signatures fall back to eager when that bound is reached. Separate graph pools allow skipped gradients and changing bucket order. A failed recording publishes no entry; loading optimizer state drops old recordings. Legacy state without the new field honours the constructor's requested mode.

    WP-198 — prepare the same training images with less exposed work¶

    The CUDA owner profile at batch 64 and 640 px attributed 113.406 ms to HSV, 9.999 ms to quantization and 5.481 ms to dequantization, before the channels-last copy. The operator trace materializes full-size intermediate batches around the existing pointwise arithmetic.

    The transfer hook now requests one Triton pixel pass for contiguous float32 CUDA images and stated float64 gains. It preserves expression order, precise tensor division, double addition before casting the saturation/value gains, nearest-even uint8 rounding and CUDA's scalar-divisor reciprocal on restore. Fused multiply-add contraction is disabled. A fresh channels-last output avoids both intermediate batches and the final layout copy. The 256-pixel launch was selected from a measured 128/256/512/1024-block matrix; it is a scheduling choice. R44 registers the optional compiler, not a method implementation.

    The existing device_hsv opt-in remains the entrance. Missing compiler/backend, unsupported devices, capture/compile scopes, noncontiguous inputs and other consumer precision keep the native path. The release guard accepts explicitly declared consumer extras only when their imports handle absence; development-group and unguarded imports still fail. No tensor, output or gain is cached.

    Without CUPTI, a two-epoch nano run spent 13.650 s waiting for the first training batches and 3.222 s across the other 62 batches. The consumer can hide part of that first-batch work: an opt-in epoch_prefetch loader resets its existing persistent iterator after fetching the final batch, so workers prepare the successor during the last step and validation. Each public iterator still ends at its own epoch. The module permits this only for complete, single-CUDA epochs with its dedicated random sampler; distributed sampling, limits, step bounds, overfit and loader reloads retain the ordinary loader.

    The shuffle generator is separate from the model's global RNG. An unused prepared epoch carries a generator-state receipt so teardown and a fresh worker pool do not skip a shuffle. A synchronous reset failure restores that state and is reported when the next epoch is requested, after the already-fetched batch has been delivered. The callback keeps a weak reference to its Trainer, and the prepared marker weakly references the existing iterator so Lightning teardown can release unused prefetched batches after an early stop. A regression through the real CombinedLoader reset catches strong retention. It creates no additional pool or executor. Queued training batches can remain resident during validation, which is why the flag is opt-in.

    The isolated L4 screening pair used 2048 public COCO2017 training images, 64 disjoint validation images, nano/B64/640/BF16 mixed, eight persistent workers and cached decoding. Each epoch is 32 training steps (2048 images at batch 64). Three complete epochs, including validation and checkpoints, took 131.179 ->119.761 s; whole processes took 138.139 ->126.883 s. All six model, optimizer, scheduler, EMA, loss and metric hashes matched. This is a screen, not the repeated headline. Five fresh-process pairs over ten epochs, alternating order with an empty compiler cache per fit, reduce median complete-fit time from 379.619 s (377.463–380.130) to 326.999 s (325.952–355.677), or 13.86%. Whole-process medians are 386.249 s (384.025–387.338) and 333.961 s (332.686–362.380), or 13.54%. The median CUDA-event steady window is 807.293 -> 727.509 ms per step. All ten fits retain identical six-state byte hashes. The slowest candidate is retained; paired fit reductions range from 6.43% to 14.09%. Strict determinism and allocation filling remain enabled. Full make gate was not run and this package remains open.

    The 13.86% headline combines two features, each measured separately: colour fusion alone saved 7.15% in the ten-epoch screen, and epoch preparation alone saved 4.50% in its three-epoch screen. A CPU CopyPaste/rasterization combination reached 7.45% with fusion and was discarded. Epoch-boundary savings depend on dataset size and validation duration; these short-fit measurements are not a full-COCO training claim.
