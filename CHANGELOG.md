# Changelog

All notable changes to lucid-yolo are documented here, following the Keep a Changelog convention; versioning is a perpetual 0.x release train — no 1.0 is ever planned, promised, or tagged — per ADR-002 (docs/DECISIONS.md).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- R21 advances from `fedde0c1` to `a5a276a` — `fuse-augmentations` 0.12, the tree upstream tags `v0.12.0` from and the capability floor the rest of Phase 14 is gated on (WP-154). No call site delegates anything yet; this row is the pin and the re-freeze it forces. `tests/fixtures/synthetic.py` imported `animal_shapes`, which upstream's restructure removed, so the bump fails at collection rather than quietly; the replacement is `tuple(AnimalShape)[:KEYPOINTS_ANIMAL_COUNT]`, which is what that helper did, read from upstream's source at the old pin rather than assumed from the name. The two generator-derived goldens are re-frozen with every one of their sixteen moved values traced to a named upstream commit before it moved: of the fifteen commits spanned, only `964fd07` and `abbf1f2` touch the generator, `964fd07` is fixture-neutral measured at the commit, and `abbf1f2` — `PrimitiveShape.TRIANGLE` equilateral again, `polygon_to_obb` re-derived in the shape's upright frame — accounts for all sixteen, returning each bit-exactly to the value frozen before `0a0cc640` introduced the obtuse-scalene triangle. Zero untraced deltas.
- Two local geometric conventions give way to `fuse-augmentations`' own, ahead of any delegation (WP-154b): `AffineParams.matrix` composes about `((W-1)/2, (H-1)/2)` rather than `(W/2, H/2)`, and every mirror (`mirror_rboxes`, `HorizontalFlip`) reflects about `(width-1)/2` rather than `width/2`. Every dependent literal is traced rather than pasted from output — `goldens/aug_invariants.json` re-frozen with the delta reproduced bit-exactly against a reverted centre, `goldens/data_checksums.json` split into its centre and mirror components by a four-way attribution run, and five point-anchored tier-A/C contract cases re-derived by hand from the new convention before being checked against the code. `goldens/frozen/0.6/aug_invariants.json`, a pure-code snapshot the deliberate convention change legitimately broke, is updated in place on a principal override of AGENTS.md §7's standing prohibition — recorded in `docs/ESCALATION.md`, not a precedent for the next one.
- The aspect-preserving letterbox and the instance keep mask now come from `fuse-augmentations` (WP-155, WP-155b). `Letterbox` keeps `inverse_map` and `forward_affine` unchanged in signature and semantics, so its six evaluation and prediction call sites are untouched, and delegates the fit, the forward matrix and the resample; `_LetterboxGeom`, `_resolve_geometry`, `_resize_pad` and both copies of `_keep_mask` are deleted. Upstream's `min_size` and `min_visibility` default to `0.0` against this project's `2.0` and `0.1`, so both are passed explicitly at all six call sites and a test per site asserts it — at the defaults even a zero-width clipped box survives. The resample is one `grid_sample` from the source canvas to the letterboxed one instead of an antialiased `F.interpolate` plus `F.pad`, which leaves the geometry bit-unchanged and moves exactly one image-derived value, `letterbox_image_mean` 0.5024 → 0.4994 against a 0.002 tolerance (upstream's opt-in antialiasing was measured and recovers nothing at this downscale). A32 is revised: non-antialiased resampling is now the whole pipeline's filter rather than the train path's deviation from the eval path. `goldens/frozen/0.6/aug_invariants.json` takes that one value on a second principal override of AGENTS.md §7, recorded in `docs/ESCALATION.md`.
- The random affine's warp is now one `fuse-augmentations` segment and `FusedAffineLetterbox` is gone (WP-156). **Breaking**: `lucid_yolo.data.FusedAffineLetterbox` is removed, along with `RandomAffine.warp_to` and `RandomAffine.apply_to`; the fused composition is now `RandomAffine(letterbox=..., allow_upscale=...)`, whose deterministic aspect-preserving fit joins the same segment so a source canvas reaches the letterboxed one in a single resample. The decomposition stays local: `_StatedGeometryAdapter` implements upstream's published `TransformAdapter` seam and answers `build_matrix` with `AffineParams.matrix`, because handing parameters to `Compose.from_params` instead would re-compose them under upstream's own decomposition — two sequential shears after the rotation against one combined shear before it, agreeing only at zero shear and diverging by up to 0.97 px on the goldens' composite case. Targets still clip and filter at the source canvas and only then map through the letterbox affine, since clipping after the letterbox would clip against the padding. Deletes `_warp_image`, `_theta_from_pixel_matrix` and `_transform_box_corners`. No golden moved: `aug_invariants` 15/15 and `data_checksums` 10/10 are bit-identical, which is also the determinism evidence — the caller's generator consumes the same draws in the same order as before the swap.

### Fixed

- `make freeze-goldens` no longer reintroduces the two generator-derived goldens WP-132 removed (WP-154c). It was a blind `cp goldens/*.json goldens/frozen/$(MINOR)/` with no notion that `fixture_checksums.json` and `data_checksums.json` are pinned to `fuse-augmentations`' output rather than to this project's own code, so WP-140 and WP-153 each re-froze them anyway, and WP-154 hit the same wall a second time. Both goldens now carry `"freezable": false`; `scripts/freeze_goldens.py` skips them at copy time, and `scripts/check_goldens.py` rejects one found under `goldens/frozen/` regardless, so the gate itself catches a recurrence rather than a human noticing it at the next release.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-09-03

### Changed

- The `[HUMAN]` roadmap tag is now `[PRINCIPAL]`, project-wide. It never meant a person rather than a machine; it meant the party who drives the project rather than the party who executes it, which is the split every use of it actually turns on. Anatomical uses of "human" are untouched, as are the pre-existing uses of "operator" meaning whoever runs the command -- the principal decides, the operator executes (WP-140b).
- D18: the one-WP-one-commit contract is a reproduction instrument and ends when the reproduction does. Once the report carries all four accepted tiers and the repository is public, a change altering no shipped behaviour, no public symbol, no golden and no documented assumption lands as an ordinary gated commit; the gate itself never relaxes, and adding a new task restarts the full procedure. Contributor admission is built as three layers, because a signature records a claim and never detects the code it is claiming about (WP-140b).
- Phase 14 is regrouped from thirteen work packages into six — five swaps and a release (WP-152b). Each row still lands as one commit, so the commits are larger and each groups swaps sharing an upstream capability and a test surface. The `FA-n` upstream dependencies are unchanged, since merging local rows merges no upstream releases: the cost is that a coarser row now waits on more upstream work before it can begin. The golden re-freeze stays alone in the one row holding no call-site swap, so the rule that a frozen value never moves beside a swap survives the merge. `_WP_FLOOR` rises from 129 to 159, which is the ratchet doing its job — the roadmap shrank, and the floor is what makes that a deliberate edit rather than a silent one.

### Fixed

- R21's registered commit is `fedde0c1`, the one `pyproject.toml` has installed since WP-132; the row had named `0a0cc640`, one upstream commit behind, since that bump (WP-146). No gate reads the register, and both hashes name real commits of the right repository, so the disagreement survived eight work packages and a release. `LetterShape`'s effect on the synthetic fixtures is recorded as measured rather than argued — both generator-derived goldens produce identical values at either commit, so WP-132's re-freeze is attributable entirely to `0a0cc640`'s triangle and centroid changes and no golden moves here.
- `tests/fixtures/synthetic.py` claimed its emitted COCO `categories` list names every animal the generator ships; it names the four geometric shapes actually drawn, and did at every pin this project has used (WP-146).

### Added

- Phase 13 (WP-141…153, release 0.6.0) and Phase 14 (WP-154…159, release 0.7.0): contributor admission, the augmentation regression guard, the keypoint ONNX export and `predict_keypoints` that 0.5.0's own entry named as owed, and then the augmentation stack moving to `fuse-augmentations`. Two phases rather than one so the guard is published before anything it guards is allowed to move (WP-140b).
- `predict_keypoints`, `KeypointPrediction`, and a keypoint branch in `lucid-predict` and `scripts/draw_predictions.py` (WP-152). `predict.py` shipped `predict_image`, `predict_segmentation` and `predict_oriented`, so a released 0.5.0 keypoint checkpoint had no shipped way to run on an image; the command's final `else` was detection, written before a fourth task existed. The composition follows `predict_segmentation`: the point stem is dense over anchors, so the decode reports each surviving row's source anchor and the points are gathered by it, with detections, indices and the dense tensor chosen from one branch in one place. Points take the same analytic letterbox inverse the boxes do, applied once to a flattened point list so no row can take a different mapping from its neighbour. `K` is read from the decoded tensor everywhere — never assumed to be COCO's 17 (A64) — and reported in the JSON `info`. The report gains a `keypoints: "xy-pairs"` key only when records carry points, so the three shipped report shapes are untouched; the layout is named because COCO's own keypoint field is a flat triplet list with a visibility flag, and nothing here predicts visibility. `draw_keypoints` takes the skeleton as a caller argument with no default edges: which points connect is dataset metadata, and baking COCO's human topology in would draw a 15-point letter schema confidently wrong.
- `KeypointExportGraph`, and the ONNX export gate now runs four tasks where it ran three (WP-151). 0.5.0 released keypoints with no exported view at all, making it the only released task whose model could not leave PyTorch. The composition follows `SegmentExportGraph` rather than `DetectExportGraph` because the point stem is dense over anchors and so needs each detection's source anchor: `decode_with_indices`, then `decode_keypoints` over the whole dense tensor, then `gather_keypoints` — decode first and gather second, the order WP-062's angle branch established and `val/oks_mAP` already uses. Unlike segmentation, a padding row is well-defined here: the shared gather clamps and zeroes it, so it yields the origin rather than the last anchor's pose. The gate's keypoint case is exported at `K = 3` rather than COCO's 17, since `K` is a constructor argument (A64) and a gate that only ran at 17 would pass a shape accidentally baked to it.
- `tests/data/test_aug_determinism.py`, the augmentation guard's fourth tier (WP-150). Six seed-keyed determinism assertions move out of `test_affine.py`, `test_fused_warp.py`, `test_mosaic.py`, `test_mixup_copypaste.py` and `test_photometric.py` and are labelled for what they are: they pin whichever sampler is installed and are re-frozen at every implementation swap, never carried across one. Separating them means a tier-D failure at a swap does not read as a regression in the behaviour tiers, where the natural re-freeze response would silently destroy a real guard. Three assertions were added rather than moved — different seeds must give *different* output, which is what catches a transform ignoring its own generator, and a flip's sixteen draws must contain both outcomes. `test_letterbox.py`'s determinism class stays where it is: it asserts purity on a transform with no generator, not reproducibility.
- `tests/data/test_aug_frozen.py` and `goldens/aug_invariants.json`, the augmentation guard's characterized tier (WP-149). Nine pytest cases state explicit parameters, assert literal geometry, and name in their own docstrings why no closed form was available — a composite of rotation, shear, scale and translation followed by a clip; a rotated box re-fitted under a shear, which unlike a rotation is not a similarity and so does not re-fit exactly; a hue rotation crossing an RGB sector boundary; a padding split resolving a half pixel by convention; a rasterised triangle whose 32 covered pixels sit against an analytic area of 34. Fifteen scalar aggregates go to the golden instead — counts exactly, image means and polygon areas within a band — because an upstream engine sampling at a different half-pixel convention would fail a byte-exact pixel expectation while being correct. Golden discovery was verified by running `check_goldens.py` directly (31/31, up from 30), not inferred from a green gate.
- `tests/data/test_aug_contract.py`, the augmentation guard's derived and invariant tiers (WP-148): twenty-two cases, no characterization values, every expectation derivable from the transform's own definition. Tier A states closed-form results — an identity affine returns its input, an integer-pixel translation is an exact shifted copy, a quarter turn maps `[1,2,3,6]` to `[2,1,6,3]`, a letterbox forward-then-inverse is the identity, two mirrors compose to the identity including the keypoint permutation, A11's half-open ring spanning `[1,4]` covers three pixels. Tier C states parameter-free invariants tying the modalities to each other — every modality on the instance axis survives or dies together, a box derived from a transported ring equals the separately transported box, rotated boxes leave every transform canonical, out-of-canvas pixels are exactly the grey fill, mixup concatenates both label sets unweighted. Coverage includes the transforms that stay in this repository.
- A public sample/apply seam on every stochastic transform (WP-147): `RandomAffine`, `FusedAffineLetterbox`, `HSVJitter`, `HorizontalFlip`, `MosaicAssembly`, `Mixup` and `CopyPaste` each gain a `sample()` that is the only method touching the generator and an `apply()` that draws nothing, with `__call__` reduced to sample-then-apply. Four new frozen parameter dataclasses join `AffineParams` on the public surface — `HSVParams`, `FlipParams`, `MosaicParams`, `MixupParams`, `CopyPasteParams` — so a transform can be exercised with *stated* parameters rather than a seed. This is what lets the augmentation guard freeze expectations that survive an implementation swap, since an upstream sampler will not draw the same numbers from the same seed. Behaviour is unchanged: every draw sequence is the original lifted verbatim, and `tests/data/test_aug_seam.py` asserted `__call__` equals `sample()` then `apply()` from the same generator state until WP-149 made it redundant and deleted it. `Letterbox` needed no change, being deterministic.
- D19 and ADR-005: the boundary between this project and `fuse-augmentations`, written down before any code moves (WP-145). `fuse` owns the pixel-and-geometry engine — matrix sampling and composition, single-pass resampling, per-modality transport, constant-fill padding, the letterbox segment and the post-warp instance filter — and `lucid-yolo` owns `Targets`, the composition order, the R1 Table S3 recipe, dataset IO, DOTA tiling, polygon rings, multi-image assemblies and everything from `assign/` inward. The rule settling the cases nobody enumerated: convention lives upstream, policy lives here. The ADR also records the five-step migration protocol, the reciprocal battle-test contract, and four conditions on promoting `fuse` to a runtime dependency, of which the first — real SemVer releases on PyPI — is an upstream commitment recorded as unconfirmed rather than as settled.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-08-25

### Added

- The fourth task, keypoints, end to end: `Targets` gains `keypoints`/`keypoint_vis` and `HorizontalFlip` a caller-supplied left/right flip-pair swap (WP-120); `CocoDetectionDataset` parses COCO's flat `keypoints` field opt-in (WP-121); `DualDetectionHead` gains a third stem emitting raw point offsets and raw R14 per-axis sigma, plus anchor-relative decode (WP-122); `RLELoss`, the only loss module in this repo carrying trainable weights — a hand-written 6-layer RealNVP flow (R14 Eq. 12) over the standardized residual, summed with a fixed unit Laplace NLL (WP-123); OKS evaluation via `faster_coco_eval.COCOeval_faster(iouType="keypoints", ...)`, since installed `torchmetrics==1.9.0` ships no keypoint `iou_type` (WP-124); and the training path composing all of it, surfacing two numerical faults no component test could see alone — the RLE residual needs the assigned box's own frame (A71), and the RealNVP coupling's raw `exp(log_scale)` diverges without RealNVP's own tanh-bounded, learned-scale form (A72). `goldens/gpu/overfit_micro_kp.json` frozen at train OKS AP 0.335667, floor set from a measured oracle/jitter probe rather than an assumed figure (WP-132).
- `lucid-eval` scores a keypoints checkpoint, which no shipped command could do: `pose_eval` reads `person_keypoints_val2017.json`, decodes both paths from one forward, and reports box AP beside the ten `oks_` OKS statistics per path (WP-134). The dispatch is the checkpoint's own `task`, as it already was for the other three, and `DualPathEvaluator` reaches the keypoint branch structurally rather than by a flag, so the other three protocols take the path they already took. A checkpoint whose point count is not COCO's 17 is refused rather than scored against a schema it does not share.
- `LaplaceNLLLoss`, and a `keypoint_loss` switch on `DetectionLitModule` that selects it in place of `RLELoss` (WP-135). It is R14 Table 7's own published control — the residual log-likelihood with the normalizing flow removed and everything else held, so a pair of runs differing only in it attributes the difference to the flow rather than to the reparameterization or the learned scale. R14 measured the pair at 67.4 AP for this arm against 70.5 for full RLE on COCO. The switch defaults to `rle`, so every existing config, checkpoint and reported figure means exactly what it meant before, and `pose_nano_smoke_laplace_nll.yaml` is `pose_nano_smoke.yaml` with the one line changed. Unlike `RLELoss` this loss carries no parameters at all. It ships as an experimental control for the pose tier's mechanism claim, not as a recommended objective.
- `val/oks_mAP`, logged every validation epoch for a `task="keypoints"` module, beside `val/mAP` rather than instead of it (WP-137). Previously a keypoints run had no per-epoch pose signal at all — `val/mAP` scored its box branch only, and OKS was computed nowhere until a standalone `lucid-eval` report ran by hand, after the fact. `hotcoco` was evaluated as a faster COCOeval backend for `module.py`'s own training-loop metrics and not adopted there (A73): benchmarked 13x faster in isolation but ~1 minute saved over a multi-hour training run — see WP-138 for where it was adopted instead.
- `lucid-eval --eval_backend {auto,hotcoco,faster_coco_eval}` (WP-138, A73): `hotcoco` as the default bbox/segm scoring engine for the standalone evaluation report, where — unlike the training loop — scoring is the whole job rather than a rounding error next to a forward pass. `auto` (the default) falls back to `faster_coco_eval` if hotcoco is not usable in the current environment (no musllinux wheel is published, per rf-detr PR #1402's own documented caveat); an explicit `--eval_backend hotcoco` raises rather than silently substituting. `faster_coco_eval` stays a required dependency and a fully-tested rollback with no reinstall — keypoints' OKS scoring and `module.py`'s per-epoch `val/mAP`/`val/segm_mAP` are untouched either way. Verified bit-for-bit against the existing engine (`tests/eval/test_coco_eval.py::TestHotcocoParity`).
- A MkDocs Material site over `docs/`, `dev`-group only — nothing in `make gate` builds it, so a contributor who never runs `mkdocs build` never installs the toolchain.
- A fourth surface on the licence audit: an undeclared licence is now a finding per dependency tier, not a silent pass.
- The Keypoint tier's acceptance record (WP-125, WP-126): a paired run, differing only in `keypoint_loss`, measured RLE against WP-135's Laplace-NLL control on real COCO human-pose data — e2e OKS AP 0.2738 against 0.2527, the same direction as R14 Table 7's own 70.5-vs-67.4 AP. `model_cards/keypoints.md` and a new `REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md` `0.5.0` section record it, matching the other three tasks; `plot_training.py` gains a fourth panel for the keypoint loss term, RLE or its control, whichever a run's `metrics.csv` names. The general docs — README, `docs/index.md`, `docs/TRAINING.md` — described a three-task project throughout and named no keypoint task at all; they now carry the fourth, framed as **keypoints rather than pose**, since `K` is a constructor argument and human pose is one instantiation of the task rather than what the architecture is for.
- `KeypointDetector`, `build_keypoint_detector`, and a frozen `goldens/params_flops_kp.json` (WP-139). The other three tasks each ship a composite module, a factory, a `.deploy()` NMS-free inference view and a size golden; keypoints shipped none of them — its model existed only as an inline call inside `DetectionLitModule`, which trains and evaluates perfectly well and leaves the release's regression contract with no entry for its own headline task. The golden is measured at detection's 640-pixel, 80-class protocol so its difference from `params_flops_det.json` is exactly the point stems' cost: +110,652 parameters and +0.1238 GFLOPs at `n`, the two figures differing by a factor of two because parameters count both head branches and FLOPs count only the deployed one. It is a golden with no published-table gate beside it, unlike its three siblings — R14 states a loss and an evaluation protocol and no architecture, so there is no size table to hold this against, and the tests say so where a fourth table constant would otherwise go. `num_keypoints` is keyword-only and required on both the class and the factory.

### Changed

- Every packaged config renamed onto one `<task>_<variant>_<tier>[_<detail>].yaml` scheme: `det_smoke.yaml`→`det_nano_smoke.yaml`, `seg_smoke.yaml`→`seg_nano_smoke.yaml`, `obb_smoke.yaml`→`obb_nano_smoke.yaml`, `pose_smoke.yaml`→`pose_nano_smoke.yaml`, `det_ablations.yaml`→`det_small_ablations.yaml` (its `variant: s`, spelled out), `overfit_100.yaml`→`det_nano_overfit_100.yaml`, `pose_smoke_ablation.yaml`→`pose_nano_smoke_laplace_nll.yaml`. `det_yolo_smoke.yaml` dropped rather than renamed — its content beyond a `data:` override and `model.num_classes` duplicated docs/DATASETS.md, so it is now a three-flag worked example in docs/TRAINING.md instead of a ninth file (WP-136).
- Checkpoint filenames carry `{task}_{variant}` ahead of Lightning's own `{epoch}-{step}` suffix, unless a config already places its own (WP-127).
- The export path collapses to one exportable decode path rather than a per-task branch.
- `mkdocs` capped below its next, unlicensed major.

### Fixed

- `CocoDetectionDataset` required a usable `segmentation` ring in every reading mode, not only `oriented=True` — a keypoints-only or plain-detect COCO export with no ring at all silently parsed to zero instances. A ring absent entirely is now fatal only for `oriented=True`; a present-but-unusable one still excludes that instance, unchanged (WP-121b).
- A keypoints checkpoint scored through the detection protocol reported roughly one eightieth of its true figure — not a decode fault but a category-count one: that protocol reads the 80-category `instances_val2017.json` regardless of the checkpoint, and COCO averages AP over every category the ground truth holds, so a person-only model's 79 empty categories each contributed zero. The keypoints protocol reads the single-category person file, and its `e2e` row is the one comparable to a training run's own `val/mAP` (WP-134).
- OKS ground truth uses each annotation's own `area`, `iscrowd`, `num_keypoints` and `bbox` instead of rebuilding them from the extent of the visible keypoints. COCO normalizes OKS by the declared area and draws the medium/large bucket boundary on it, and ignores an instance on the declared `num_keypoints`, so the reconstruction was scoring a different protocol under the same name. Ground truth carrying no such metadata — every synthetic fixture, and WP-124's one-shot shape — keeps the reconstruction and the numbers it already produced (WP-134).

## [0.4.0] - 2026-08-15

### Added

- Single-image inference for all three tasks, as a library call and as the fourth console script `lucid-predict`: `predict_image` for detections, `predict_segmentation` for instance masks and `predict_oriented` for rotated boxes, each answering in the original image's coordinates through the letterbox inverse rather than in canvas coordinates. The three refuse each other by name — `forward` returns a `DualHeadOutput` for every task, so a segmentation checkpoint handed to the detection path produced boxes with its mask branch never consulted — and `--ema`, `--device` and `--output` keep `lucid-eval`'s spellings, so the two commands cannot disagree about what a flag means (WP-089, WP-090, WP-091).

- `RotatedNMSDecoder`: the oriented tier now has the suppression baseline the axis-aligned tier has reported since 0.1, greedy and class-wise over the one-to-many branch by the exact polygon `rotated_iou`, on canonicalized boxes. `predict_oriented(decoder="nms")` decodes where it used to raise, on an unchanged signature — the refusal WP-091 shipped is lifted rather than worked around (WP-091b, A24).

- `merge_whole_images`: tile detections are un-letterboxed, translated by the window origin recorded at tiling time and scored against whole-image ground truth reassembled from the tiles' own annotations, with seam duplicates resolved by core ownership rather than by suppression — an NMS-free path has no suppression stage in which to drop the duplicate. Every oriented figure this project published before this was a per-tile figure, and a per-tile score never pays the duplicate-detection cost a whole-image score charges (WP-107, A53, A59).

- A YOLO-format dataset reader: a `data.yaml` plus a `labels/<split>/*.txt` tree of normalized rows produces the same `Targets` container the COCO path does, so assignment, loss and metric code is untouched. Oriented rows arrive as eight normalized polygon coordinates and are canonicalized to the long-edge convention on load (WP-099, A54–A56).

- `DetectionDataModule` dispatches on layout: a YOLO root trains through `lucid-yolo fit` and not only through a library call, the reader chosen by which layout `data_root` actually satisfies rather than by a flag the operator has to remember (WP-099b).

- The pre-run check learns the YOLO tree, and an unstated `--dataset` probes instead of defaulting to COCO: pointed at a YOLO root with no flag it used to report a missing `train2017` for a tree `fit` trains on without complaint. The probe is `detect_layout`, the same one the datamodule dispatches on, so the pre-flight resolves a root exactly as the run does or fails the same way (WP-099c).

- `check_dataset(splits=...)`, threaded into both root checkers. A third-party export shipping only `train` — a correct export whose validation split was never cut — failed a pre-flight on a `val` nobody had promised. An empty `splits` is refused rather than honoured, since checking no split would report a pass over a root nothing had been read from (WP-099e).

- An export gate on the deployed one-to-one graph, all three heads: the exported ONNX carries no `NonMaxSuppression` node and does carry the `TopK` that stands in for suppression, at the static output shape `TopKDecoder` documents, and run under onnxruntime it reproduces the checkpoint-loaded module's own decode — class ids exactly, boxes to 4.6e-05. Nothing ships: `onnx` and `onnxruntime` are dev-group only, the export itself being `torch.onnx.export` (WP-066, A9, A23, A30, A37, A45).

- A third surface on the licence audit: the files each wheel's `RECORD` declares it installed, matched by filename against a named table of copyleft native libraries. A wheel can declare a permissive licence, ship licence documents that name no copyleft anywhere, and vendor a GPL binary regardless — `av`, which `supervision` hard-requires, ships `libx264` under BSD-3-Clause metadata, and both older checks pass it clean. The check narrows that hole rather than closing it, and says so: a library the table does not name passes exactly as `av` did (WP-109, D16).

- `scripts/draw_predictions.py`, the release's worked example: a checkpoint and an image to a figure showing what the model answered — boxes for a `detect` checkpoint, boxes and per-instance mask overlays for a `segment` one, rotated quadrilaterals for an `obb` one rather than their upright envelopes, which are a different rectangle from the one the model reported. The drawing functions take an already-computed prediction rather than a checkpoint, which is what makes the geometry assertable in a repository that ships no trained weights (D14). `matplotlib` stays a `dev` dependency: nothing under `src/lucid_yolo/` imports it, so a wheel a consumer installs pulls no plotting stack. The roadmap row named `supervision`, which is refused — it hard-requires `av`, whose wheel ships a GPL `libx264` under BSD-3-Clause metadata (WP-067, D16).

### Changed

- `rotated_iou` and the seven private helpers it exclusively owns move from `eval/dota_eval.py` to `data/rotated_geom.py`. WP-091b had put an evaluation module on the decode path's import graph for a function that is pure geometry with no evaluation content (WP-091c).

- One rotated-box shape check where `rotated_geom.py` carried two. Their conditions were character-identical and differed only in the letter naming the row count: `rotated_iou`'s signature distinguishes `(M, 5)` from `(N, 5)` because the result is `(M, N)`, but the predicate each operand satisfies is the same one, so a second letter in the rejection named a difference the check never tested (WP-091d).

- `losses/probiou.py`'s own shape guard is pinned as a contract rather than folded into that one: it accepts arbitrary leading dimensions where `rotated_geom` requires exactly 2-D, which is deliberate — the loss is elementwise in its leading shape and its in-tree caller passes `(P, 5)` from the assignment (WP-091e, A19).

### Removed

- `lucid-download` is removed, as 0.3.0 said it would be: `lucid-data download` is the only spelling. ## [0.3.0] - 2026-08-14

### Added

- Long-edge rotated-box primitives: canonicalization to `w >= h` with the angle on `[-45, 135)` degrees, quadrilateral conversion in both directions, and a vectorized point-in-rotated-rect test. Three conventions the paper leaves open are fixed here and inherited by the whole oriented path — the angle turns `+x` towards `+y`, containment is edge-inclusive, and an exact square folds towards zero (WP-055).
- DOTA-v1.0 label parsing: the 15 categories in the published order, metadata headers skipped, category names normalized across their hyphenated and underscored spellings, and each annotated quadrilateral converted to a canonical long-edge box. Rotated boxes are kept on the same instance axis as the axis-aligned envelopes and labels, so one mask filters every modality (WP-056).
- `make check-data DATASET=dota` — DOTA root layout, image/label pairing and published-count validation beside the existing COCO path (WP-056).
- Overlapping 1024 px crop tiling for aerial imagery, with the source paper's partial-object rule: an instance clipped to under 70% of its area is flagged difficult rather than dropped, and re-fitted to a long-edge box. Crop overlap is a parameter; the visible fraction is carried alongside each instance (WP-057).
- Rotated-aware augmentation: random affine, mosaic and mixup now carry rotated boxes instead of refusing them, warping each box through its four corners and re-fitting a long-edge box — exact under a similarity, an explicit fit under shear (WP-058).
- ProbIoU rotated-box loss: both forms the source paper proposes, the bounded Hellinger distance and the unbounded Bhattacharyya distance, evaluated in a cancellation-free form that holds float32 accuracy from square boxes out to 1000:1 elongation and stays finite on degenerate input (WP-059).
- Square-object angle loss: the auxiliary double-angle term that resolves what the rotated IoU loss cannot, weighted towards near-square targets by a log-Gaussian in the aspect ratio and zero at every quarter turn, so a square is never penalized for choosing either of its two indistinguishable orientations. The two representatives a square can arrive as score bit-identically, not merely to tolerance (WP-060).
- Rotated candidate selection in the Task-Aligned Assigner and its STAL subclass: an oriented ground truth now decides candidacy by point-in-rotated-rect containment instead of by its axis-aligned envelope, through one optional argument that leaves the accepted axis-aligned path bit-identical when omitted. - Oriented detection head and NMS-free rotated decode: a third stem on each head branch predicts the angle directly, with the squashing nonlinearity of the previous versions removed, and the decode turns the ordinary box regression about its own centre and normalizes to the long-edge convention. An angle of zero reproduces the axis-aligned box exactly, and the two raw angles a half turn apart decode to the same box rather than two. Enabling the branch leaves the shipped detection head bit-identical, verified across 684 parameter and forward digests (WP-062).
- Rotated mAP50-95 for oriented detection: exact polygon-intersection IoU against a shapely oracle, the COCO threshold grid and 101-point interpolation, and the DOTA convention where an instance flagged difficult is neither credited nor penalized. The detection cap follows what the model emits rather than COCO's smaller default, so no part of the output is discarded and then counted as missed (WP-063).
- The license audit now reads the license documents a wheel bundles, not only the fields it declares. ### Fixed

- Validation no longer leaves the accelerator idle. The loader decodes every tile once per epoch and mosaic assembles four source tiles per sample, so a 64-image step at 1024 px is around 250 decodes and PNG is where a smoke run starves. PNG stays the default — a tile is an exact crop of a lossless source — and the annotations are identical either way. A full DOTA build is 1,869 multi-megapixel decodes and it ran silently on one core, so an operator could not distinguish a working build from a hung one while the rest of the machine idled. Work is dispatched per source image, one worker per CPU by default and `--workers` to choose, each pinned to a single torch thread; `--progress false` turns the bar off. - `lucid-data check --dataset dota` no longer fails on a correct download. It compared the summed `train` and `val` counts against the published 2,806 images and 188,282 instances by default, and those describe DOTA-v1.0 whole: the source paper takes half the images as training, a sixth as validation and a third as testing, and releases ground truth for the first two only, so an annotated root holds about two thirds of them and can never sum to them. The totals are now reported on a `NOTE` line and compared only where the caller states one, with `--expected_images`, `--expected_instances` and `--expected_classes` to assert them once a provisioning's counts are known; the layout, pairing and parse checks are unchanged, and an expectation aimed at another layout raises rather than being ignored (WP-097).
- The angle term's weight is now measured rather than assumed: `0.25`, down from the `1.0` this project had picked for a gain R1 never states (A22). At `1.0` the term did not merely over-weight the objective, it destabilised angle regression on elongated targets — across five seeds the oriented overfit cleared its floor once, with a run-to-run spread of 0.667 and a worst run at 0.31; at `0.25` it clears four times with a spread of 0.096, and the mean angular error on elongated targets halves. The cause is visible in the term itself: `sin²(2Δθ)` is zero at both zero and a quarter turn, so for an elongated box a 90-degree error scores as a perfect answer and only the rotated IoU term objects (WP-093, A22).
- A gate on the roadmap's own table shape: an unescaped pipe inside a code span opens a cell, so a row renders its tail into the wrong columns and drops the overflow. Two rows had already shipped that way, and neither existing gate could see it — one reads only the first column, the other matches the last (WP-093).
- Exact recall sampling for the detection instrument: `evaluate_bbox` now supplies the 101-point recall grid as correctly rounded hundredths instead of accepting the metric's float32 default, which overshot `k/100` at 36 of the 101 indices. A class whose recall landed exactly on one of those boundaries forfeited that point and `1/101` of its average precision, always downward — an ordinary case rather than an exotic one, since a class with 5, 10, 20, 25, 50 or 100 ground truths lands on a grid point at every recall it can attain. Both mAP instruments now forfeit no boundary, and agree exactly where they used to differ (WP-092, A46).
- The validation worker count fits the host again. WP-102 let a named `--data.num_workers` reach the val loader and removed the guard rather than the silence: at 1024 px a batch of 64 stacked images is 805 MB, so a named 32 queued around 51 GB for validation alone, beside a training pool still resident at the epoch boundary, and the host died. A count inherited from a named training count now passes the shared-memory budget, measured when the val loader is built so the training queue is already counted, and says so with the number and the override flag. A count stated as `--data.val_num_workers` is capped by nothing, which is where an operator asks for the whole machine (WP-103).
- The oriented epoch boundary no longer stalls for a minute after validation reaches 100%. Scoring 10,000 tiles at 300 detections each measured 178 s, 58% of it in the matcher: the ten IoU thresholds share the overlap matrix and the score order and differ only in what counts as a hit, so ten passes re-walked the same detections to consume different subsets of the same ground truths. The availability state is now carried per threshold and the walk happens once, and detections that reach no ground truth at the lowest threshold skip it entirely — they cannot score, cannot be discarded and consume nothing at any threshold, which on an early-epoch model is nearly the whole loop. 178 s to 38.6 s, with every pinned value unchanged (WP-104).
- `lucid-eval --output` creates its parent directory instead of raising after the scoring pass has finished and printed its numbers, which turned minutes of accelerator work into a traceback. Both evaluation paths also draw a progress bar now: the length is known ahead of time, and four minutes of silence is indistinguishable from a hang (WP-105).
- Horizontal flip left rotated boxes outside the long-edge angle range: it negated the angle without re-wrapping, so any box past 45 degrees came out non-canonical (WP-058).
